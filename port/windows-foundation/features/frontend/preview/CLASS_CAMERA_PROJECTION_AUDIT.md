# Class-select camera and ground coverage audit

## Result

No source-demonstrated camera projection, camera-parent, viewport, or culling mismatch explains the lower ground gaps in the current capture of the local class-selection asset. With the source target in its non-rotated orientation, the frontend camera and the original programmable-GL camera produce the same clip coordinates for the same world point after projection fixup. The local v1.0.2 scene has no source triangles at the previously measured uncovered sample points in the settled Knight/Rogue views. Rendering an alternate camera phase moves the gaps but does not supply the missing ground.

## Source and implementation evidence

- The local scene is `models/class_selection.bdae`, SHA-256 `87303c458be43bafca5b1d717136b679d4f70623545ca1fea20841aa57033de9` (the local v1.0.2 asset; the reference footage is v1.0.3).
- Original `CCameraSceneNode::recalculateProjectionMatrix` at `0058364c.c` calls `buildProjectionMatrixPerspectiveFov` at `00582c88.c`. The original builder writes `f/aspect`, `f`, `far/(far-near)`, `+1`, and `-near*far/(far-near)` into the perspective matrix. The actual GL driver's `CCommonGLDriverBase::fixUpProjectionMatrix` at `006dd9d0.c` changes the depth terms to `2*far/(far-near)-1` and `-2*near*far/(far-near)`. Its orientation fixup is in `005a8f88.c`.
- The original camera view builder `buildCameraLookAtMatrix` at `00582e64.c` uses the same right/up rows as the frontend's `cameraViewMatrix`; the original camera has positive-forward view Z while the frontend has negative-forward view Z. The paired source-LH/source-GL and frontend-RH/OpenGL Z signs yield equal `xclip`, `yclip`, `zclip`, and `wclip` with the standard non-rotated target orientation. `fixUpProjectionMatrixOrientation` at `005a8f88.c` can rotate/flip matrices for a target orientation, but the available app snapshot does not record that live orientation field; no orientation change is inferred here.
- Original Collada camera construction `CColladaCameraSceneNode` at `006e5550.c` converts serialized horizontal FOV to vertical FOV with `2*atan(tan(horizontal/2)/serializedAspect)`. The class camera serializes horizontal FOV `74.08049774169922` degrees and aspect `1.5`; `ClassPreviewScene::sample` uses that conversion. The base scene camera keeps aspect bits `0x3faaaaab` (4:3), and original `RenderClassSelectPane` at `00428b74.c` changes the physical viewport without changing that camera aspect. `Renderer::applyCamera` uses the equivalent vertical-FOV/aspect pair.
- `CColladaCameraSceneNode::onRegisterSceneNode` at `006e5238.c` uses an attached target node when present, otherwise transforms local `(0,0,-100)` through the camera world matrix. This BDAE's `Camera01-camera` target URI is empty. The source graph evidence `class-scene-layout-v48.json` places `Camera01-node` under `dummy_camera_angle-node` under `root_Camera-node`; `ClassPreviewScene::sample` uses the animated camera node's composed world matrix for eye and transformed local target. Its up vector is the source `Show428f38` value `(0,0,1)`.
- `MenuCharacterSelect::Show` at `00428f38.c` sets camera up, near/far `50/50000`, and local position `(0,0,-200)`, then binds the animator. `Update` at `00428498.c`, `SetAnim` at `00428078.c`, and `UpdateAnim` at `00427ffc.c` show that the animator samples the current source cursor, adds `Application::GetDt()`, and clamps to the clip end. The frontend passes real elapsed milliseconds to `ClassPreviewScene::sample`, which samples before advancing/clamping, then renders that camera. It rebases its elapsed clock after blocking class asset loading.
- `RenderClassSelectPane` obtains the authored `class_select` absolute bounds, divides by the root inverse pixel scales, sets the resulting viewport, calls `SceneManager::drawAll`, and restores the old viewport. Existing viewport evidence identifies the current matched 16:9 source rectangle as the full physical window; the camera remains 4:3 in both paths.
- The original BDAE contains two visible mesh instances, three draw ranges, and 2,273 triangles. The current adapter decodes those exact instances/ranges and draws them without frustum culling. Source scene culling could omit out-of-frustum nodes but cannot create triangles in uncovered pixels. Existing coverage probes report zero source triangles at the gap points; `SCENE_COVERAGE_PROOF.md` records the all-range and front-face counts.

## Phase and visual checks

Reused `backdrop_transition_probe.exe` against `.local-inputs/windows-main-frontend-v1/assets`. The Knight camera moves from `(0,-1354.82,175.21)` at initial phase to `(-325.416,-1315.16,175.21)` at 250 ms, `(-677.161,-1173.46,175.21)` at 650 ms, and `(-870.864,-1037.86,175.21)` by 1,300 ms. The 6000–6032 ms `lol_1_idle` range keeps the settled camera pose. Ground mesh world-matrix delta is zero at sampled transition/idle checkpoints; the dedicated idle probe found zero delta at every integer millisecond from 6000 through 6032. The transition phase changes which bottom sample points are covered, but the sampled phase points never cover the left, center, and right bottom probes together. The settled gap is not explained by a missing idle update.

I extracted the local v1.0.3 reference video `Dungeon Hunter 2 (v1.0.3) Part 1 [720p] [z_Zky7qQdYs].mp4` at 50, 52, and 54 seconds to `.local-inputs/frontend-ground-audit/original-{50,52,54}s.png`. These are direct visual evidence that the reference's bottom ground is filled while its class-selection page is open. They do not prove its elapsed time since `ClassSelect::Show`; that transition time is not visible in the sampled frames.

## Boundary and next test

The remaining demonstrated difference is asset/version scope: the local v1.0.2 BDAE's projected triangles leave gaps, while the v1.0.3 video shows ground at the same screen area. No v1.0.3 `CLASS_SELECTION.bdae` or native camera-state receipt is available in the inspected workspace, so the footage cannot yet be matched to a source clip cursor or scene hash. The next decisive test is to obtain the v1.0.3 scene asset and linked camera/scene data, or capture a native v1.0.3 run with scene hash, clip name/cursor, camera world matrix, viewport rectangle, and target-orientation value logged at a matched frame. No FOV, aspect, viewport, scene geometry, or compositing edit is justified by the available evidence.
